How I Discovered My Favorite Recipe

My favorite recipe wasn’t from a cookbook—it started as a happy accident on a quiet evening with a few simple ingredients. I only had rice, tomatoes, onions, and leftover chicken. No measurements, no rules—just curiosity.

The moment it clicked

I let sliced onions brown slowly until sweet, added chopped tomatoes with a pinch of salt, and simmered until the sauce thickened. I stirred in the chicken, folded in the rice, and let everything steam. The aroma hit first—rich, warm, and comforting. That’s how my “sunset rice” was born.

What the dish taught me

  • Patience creates flavor: Low, slow heat turned onions into gold.
  • Taste > exact numbers: Adjusting by taste beat strict measurements.
  • Simple can be special: Common ingredients, memorable results.

Refining over time

Each time I made it, I changed one thing—more chili, a dash of lime, fresh herbs, or a handful of vegetables. Bit by bit, I found balance: gentle heat, bright acidity, and a cozy, tender finish.

Cook’s tip: Keep a tiny notebook (or your Notes app) near the stove. Jot what you changed. Tiny tweaks become your signature.

Why it’s still my favorite

It’s quick, forgiving, and full of feeling. Every spoonful reminds me that the best recipes don’t just feed you—they tell a story about where you were and what you had the courage to try.

“Sunset Rice” — ingredients

  • 1 cup cooked rice (any type you like)
  • 2 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 cup shredded cooked chicken (or beans for a veg option)
  • Salt, black pepper, and chili to taste
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Optional: fresh herbs, a squeeze of lime, colorful veggies

Simple steps

  1. Sauté onions in oil over medium-low heat until golden.
  2. Add tomatoes + salt; cook until saucy and slightly sweet.
  3. Stir in chicken and spices; warm through.
  4. Fold in rice gently; cover and steam 2–3 minutes.
  5. Finish with herbs or lime. Serve warm.

Now I don’t measure—I listen to the sizzle, smell the sauce, and stop when it feels right. That’s how I discovered my favorite recipe: with intuition, patience, and a little courage to improvise.
